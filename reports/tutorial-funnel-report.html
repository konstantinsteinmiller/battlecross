#178 — Why players leave around the opening boss, and what keeps them

Structured browser report. Generated 2026-11-02 from a real-browser walkthrough of the first visit (scripts/tutorial-funnel.mjs), the code paths around the boss and its chest, and a web search on first-session retention. Raw data: tf-desktop/funnel.json, tf-phone/funnel.json, tf-landscape/funnel.json (+ screenshots per step).

1. Bottom line

The strongest, code-confirmed cause of "95 % leave right before the boss or right after the boss chest" is a finish line in the wrong place: before #177, the moment the boss fell the goal became "Take your loot and leave" (the goal chip steps aside for the Leave button itself, which shows the chest count), the chest sat right there, and the way on (the teachers' camp, the cage, the broken caravan) was behind rock with no cue at all. Opening the chest was the natural last action, and Leave was the only offered next action. That is a player-built exit ramp.

Fixed and seen working in the browser (desktop and phone portrait):

  • Leave stays hidden until the teachers are out of the cage.
  • New goal chip "Free the caged teachers" from the second the boss is down.
  • Gold chevrons lead from the player to the key, then the cage, and the way is strewn with wheels and splintered spokes that lead to the broken caravan.

What is not known: the real reason a particular Poki player closes the tab. Nothing here reads real player data. To find out, the first visit now reports its own funnel (section 6), so the next Poki playtest answers it directly.

4. Findings

F1 — Exit ramp after the boss (cause, fixed). Old flow: boss down → goal "Take your loot and leave" (shown as the Leave button with its chest count). The camp (cage, teachers, fire, caravan) lies behind the boss's floor with nothing pointing at it. Fixed by #177 (see section 1). Evidence: screenshots /06-boss-down.png, /08-finale-chest-open.png.

F2 — The camp's story is invisible from the chest (cause, fixed). Players had no reason to look past the chest. Wheels, spokes and the caravan now form a visible trail that ends at the wagon; the chevrons make it unmissable. Evidence: tf-phone/phone/08-finale-chest-open.png shows the chevrons, the goal chip, a wheel and spokes, and the camp fire in one phone-portrait frame.

F3 — No ad stands in the way (ruled out). adPacing.breakAllowed blocks the interstitial after the opening fight, after defeats and before decisions, so the result window → Continue → map path has no ad on the first visit. This was checked in code, not in the Poki SDK.

F4 — The map after the first clear gives three calls to action (partly fixed). The result window says "New on the map: Goblin Hollows, Whispering Woods", the map marks both with glowing NEW tags, and the goal chip says "Travel to Sunford" (where the teachers and the smith are). On phone portrait the whole realm is squeezed into a ~390×220 strip with about 15 overlapping labels (tf-phone/phone/12-map.png). Done: the goal's place now gets the same bobbing arrow a sought teacher's place has. Not done: see recommendation R2.

F5 — The result window is sound in portrait and desktop (checked). It fits 390×844 and 1280×720 without scrolling, the Continue button is inside the viewport, and the new-gear rows have clear Equip buttons.

F7 — In phone landscape the result window hides what makes a player want to go on (new). At 844×390 the window shows the stats and Continue (tf-landscape/landscape/11-results.png); the two gear rows with their Equip buttons and the "New on the map" line are below the fold, inside a scroll. A landscape player sees the stats and a green Continue, and nothing about the reward or the next place. Recommendation R3 should therefore also move the next-step line above the stats on short screens. The camp scene itself is fine in landscape: the chevrons, the goal chip and the wheel trail are all in frame (tf-landscape/landscape/08-finale-chest-open.png).

F6 — Funnel was blind (fixed). Nothing recorded where a leaving player stopped. See section 6.

5. What the research says (web search, 2026-11-02)

Practitioner sources, not controlled evidence; none is specific to Poki.

  • End the first experience on a win and a clear next goal, and show what unlocks next (Unity: 10 FTUE tips, PocketGamer.biz on FTUE).
  • Find the step with the largest loss with a funnel through the first session, then playtest that step (devtodev, bugnet).
  • Confusing onboarding, an early difficulty wall and a slow start are the usual culprits; removing one step or one click can matter (bugnet).
  • Place ads at completion/failure moments rather than interrupting play (juegostudio).
  • Successful run-based adventure games (Archero, Survivor.io) pay out at the boss and put the choice of power right there: Archero offers a blessing before the first big boss and a skill-for-health gamble after it (urgametips, easegame). The reward and the "what next" come from the same screen.

9. #181 — A depth preview of the class diversity without overwhelming a new player

The situation. 16 base classes (4 taught openly in Sunford, 4 in Silverbridge, 4 secret ones found through a master's test, 4 more taught openly) plus 4 prestige hybrids; a hero may learn skills from any class (8 active and 4 passive slots), and every class now has six locked endgame skills. That is a lot of depth, and the right amount of it for a player who stays. For a Poki player in minute three, any of it shown as a list is noise.

Principles (from the research in section 5 and the funnel above).

  • Show depth by doing, not by listing: one thing to look at, one to try.
  • Never more than three choices on screen at once; reveal the rest as a curiosity gap ("more teachers wait in Silverbridge"), not a catalogue.
  • The preview should end in a decision the player owns, because choosing a style is the first hook of a build game (Archero offers a blessing before the first big boss and a skill choice after it).
  • Every preview must cost the player zero extra taps on the path they are already on.

10. #171 — Healing shamans and a fire-pit boss: does it help retention or overwhelm a Poki player?

What it asks. (a) A boss whose first phase is destroying healing shamans, so the boss is not constantly out-healing the hero; (b) a harder fight where the player kites the boss onto fire pits that pressure plates arm, each activation costing the boss 10 % of its health, the boss leaping out so one activation cannot grill it.

What the game already has. 13 scripted boss modules (centaur, chimera, cyclops, elder dragon, hellgate, hydra, leviathan, shatter lake, skyfire, sphinx, tusker and others), pressure plates and plate puzzles in the level generator, and a mercy rule that cuts boss damage when the first fight goes badly. So the infrastructure exists; the question is fit.

Assessment.

(a) Healing shamans (kill the healers first)(b) Kite the boss onto plate-armed fire pits
Retention valuehigh: a clear, readable rule ("break the green beams, then the boss"), a classic that casual players learn in one attempt; fixes a real frustration (a boss that out-heals a weak build)high for those who master it, low for a first session: it is a skill-expression fight
Overwhelm risk on Pokilow if telegraphed (green beams from each shaman to the boss, the boss shielded while one lives)high on touch: kiting needs fine stick control while watching plates and pits; a new player has two new ideas at once (plates arm pits, the boss must stand in them)
Fit to the first minutesnot in the tutorial boss; fine from the second or third zonenever before the player has beaten two bosses
Failure modea boss that is untouchable until the adds die and no cue says soan unwinnable-feeling fight; players quit at exactly the point #178 is trying to fix

Verdict. Yes to (a): it is the better retention play and the lower risk. (b) is a good optional depth fight, but as a mandatory kill for Poki's mostly casual, often-touch audience it would raise the quit rate. Build it so the boss can be beaten without the pits (they are a big damage bonus and a mastery bonus, not the only way), place it after the first two zones, and give it a one-line, one-time hint in the fight ("Lure it onto the fire!") rather than a lesson. Ship (a) first, read the boss_seen → boss_down and hero_fell events per boss in the next Poki playtest, and only then decide on (b). Not built here: this is an assessment; no boss was changed.
